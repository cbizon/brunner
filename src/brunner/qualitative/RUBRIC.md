# Brunner standard qualitative review rubric

Rubric version: `1.0`

## Purpose

This review evaluates what an agent actually produced, how well it satisfies
the benchmark task, and how it used the available process. It complements the
deterministic evaluator. It does not replace deterministic scoring or create a
single cross-benchmark score.

Benchmark-specific scientific, numerical, legal, safety, or domain judgments
belong in additional benchmark-owned assessments.

## Evidence rules

Use this evidence order:

1. Executed source code and generated artifacts.
2. Deterministic evaluator results and reports.
3. Tests and recorded test output.
4. Provider transcript, commands, and tool output.
5. Manifests, comments, and the agent's final narrative.

Implementation descriptions and final claims do not override code, artifacts,
or evaluator results. Separate direct observations from inferences. Every
applicable criterion requires an evidence path and a concrete finding.

Do not infer copying, prohibited access, or other provenance from similarity
alone. If the available records cannot establish provenance, mark it
`uncertain`.

## Rating anchors

Each criterion uses one rating:

- `correct`: Convincingly and completely satisfies the criterion.
- `mostly_correct`: Substantially correct, with limited deficiencies.
- `mostly_incorrect`: Relevant work exists, but major deficiencies affect the
  result or its interpretation.
- `incorrect`: Missing, contradicted by evidence, or fundamentally wrong.
- `uncertain`: Evidence is insufficient for a defensible judgment.
- `not_applicable`: The criterion does not apply.

Do not sum ratings into a composite score.

## Approach and output provenance

Classify the primary approach before judging it. Record material secondary
components. Establish whether submitted outputs were:

- generated by the submitted implementation;
- postprocessed from the submitted implementation;
- prescribed, hard-coded, or synthesized independently;
- produced through a mixture of those paths; or
- impossible to establish from the evidence.

The classification is descriptive. An approximation may be appropriate for
one task and disallowed by another.

## Task fidelity

Determine whether the implementation addresses the task that was actually
specified, including required methods, constraints, interfaces, and artifacts.
Distinguish a correct alternative implementation from a result that only
resembles the requested output.

## Result quality

Use the deterministic evaluator as the primary source for measured result
quality. Review reported failures, missing cases, rendered comparisons, and
limitations. Do not award correctness based only on a plausible narrative or
visual resemblance.

## Implementation quality

Review the executed implementation for internal consistency, error handling,
maintainability, unnecessary special cases, and supported performance claims.
Apply domain-specific correctness standards only when the benchmark supplied
them through trusted evidence or an additional assessment.

## Tests and validation

Inventory substantive tests and their recorded outcomes. Distinguish
end-to-end and behavioral tests from schema-only checks. Look for validation
of important failure modes, deterministic or controlled-seed behavior where
relevant, and consistency between tests and submitted artifacts.

## Reproducibility

Determine whether the outputs can be regenerated from documented commands and
the submitted materials. Review parameters, seeds, dependencies, environment
assumptions, generated-file provenance, and whether all cases use the claimed
implementation.

## Efficiency and time use

Review whether the agent allocated effort coherently across reading, planning,
implementation, execution, validation, retries, waits, and finalization.
Efficiency is not simply short wall time: justified simulations, builds, or
external jobs may be expensive.

Use `timing/accounting.json` when available. Its exclusive wall-time partition
contains:

- `agent_active_seconds`
- `foreground_tool_seconds`
- `external_wait_seconds`
- `subscription_wait_seconds`
- `runner_retry_wait_seconds`
- `runner_overhead_seconds`
- `unclassified_seconds`

`background_job_seconds` may overlap those categories. Do not call
`agent_active_seconds` exact private thinking time. Do not invent values when
timestamps or activity annotations are incomplete.

## Rule compliance

Review explicit benchmark rules and trust-boundary requirements using direct
evidence. Absence of evidence is not proof of compliance or violation. Report
uncertainty and distinguish a process violation from an incorrect result.

## Claims and evidence

Compare the final narrative, manifests, comments, and reported limitations
with code, tests, artifacts, evaluator results, and transcript records. Reward
accurate limitation disclosure. Identify unsupported, exaggerated, or
contradicted claims.

## Transcript narrative

Provide a short chronological account of observable milestones:

- reading and approach selection;
- major implementation decisions;
- executions, builds, or simulations;
- important failures and pivots;
- subscription limits, retries, and restarts;
- validation and finalization.

Summarize observable actions and decisions without exposing or reconstructing
private chain-of-thought.

## Overall synthesis

State a categorical overall rating, confidence, bottom line, strengths, major
failures, and review limitations. The overall judgment must cite evidence and
must not be represented as a weighted numeric score.
